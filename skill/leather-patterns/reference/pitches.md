# Pricking-iron pitches and thread

| Pitch | Name | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 2.7 mm | fine | small watch straps, lacing, delicate work |
| 3.0 mm | fine | card holders, small accessories |
| 3.38 mm | Japanese / "diamond" | wallets, general leathercraft (very common) |
| 3.85 mm | European | wallets, bags, heavier goods (very common) |
| 4.0 mm | European coarse | belts, straps, large panels |
| 5.0 mm | extra coarse | heavy straps, saddlery |

**Pick one and never mix.** Pitch is baked into every pattern; a 3.38 mm pattern
cannot be punched with a 3.85 mm iron.

## Thread pairing

| Pitch | Thread |
|---|---|
| 3.0–3.38 mm | 0.35–0.45 mm waxed |
| 3.85 mm | 0.45–0.55 mm waxed |
| 4.0–5.0 mm | 0.6–0.8 mm waxed |

Thick thread at a fine pitch puckers the edge and will not lay flat in the hole.

## Stitch-line offset

| Method | Offset from edge |
|---|---|
| hand saddle stitch | 3–4 mm |
| machine stitch | 5–6 mm |

Add roughly half the hole diameter to get the centreline.

## Hole diameter

Hole size should match the iron: a 3.85 mm iron typically takes a 1.0–1.2 mm hole.
The pattern draws holes at r = 0.5 mm; adjust if your awl is larger.
