# Treatments — how leather gets its properties

A hide leaves the beamhouse as almost nothing: an empty collagen scaffold with the fats, hairs
and non-structural proteins stripped out. **Everything a maker recognises as "this leather" is
put back deliberately, in four windows.**

This folder is about those windows: **every step, every chemistry, and what each one does to the
material.** The animals and their anatomy are in `../anatomy/`. This is the other half — the
half where a tannery decides whether a hide becomes a sole, a jacket or a phone case.

---

## The four windows

| Window | What happens | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Beamhouse** | soak, unhair, lime, flesh, split, delime, bate, pickle | **stretch, softness ceiling, grain tightness.** Set here, cannot be recovered later |
| **2 · Tanning** | the stabilising chemistry | **heat resistance, water uptake, how it ages, whether it patinas** |
| **3 · Post-tanning** | neutralise, retan, dye, fatliquor, dry, mechanical | **temper (firm/soft), colour, break, area yield** |
| **4 · Finishing** | coating, plating, embossing, buffing, lacquer | **surface durability, stain resistance, appearance, breathability** |

## The one idea this folder is built on: zero-sum levers

**Almost every treatment that improves one property degrades another.** This is not a
limitation of bad tanneries; it is the physics of the material. Four examples that hold across
the whole industry:

| Lever | Improves | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **More fatliquor, deeper penetration** | softness | **coarser break** — the grain loosens as the fibre structure is lubricated apart ✓ |
| **Vegetable retan** | firmness, body, patina | flexibility, water resistance, time |
| **Chrome tan** | softness, water resistance, speed | patina, repairability, environmental load |
| **Heavy pigment coating** | stain and wear resistance | **breathability** — it becomes a vapour barrier |
| **Buffing the grain (nubuck)** | velvet hand | the outer protective layer, and with it stain resistance |
| **Milling / staking** | softness, grain character | dimensional stability, grain tightness |
| **Making it transparent** | novelty | **strength and wet performance** — a documented, unresolved trade-off |

**So the useful question is never "is this treatment good?" but "what did this treatment buy,
and what did it pay for it?"** `08-property-matrix.md` is that question in table form.

## What "complete" means here, and what it does not

This folder tries to cover **every step, every chemistry, every dye class and every finish that
changes the material**, plus the branches that are not steps at all — hair-on (a beamhouse that
skips a step), the false friends (materials that are not leather), the regulatory layer, and the
reverse index.

It does **not** give recipes, times or percentages for anything proprietary. Those are
tannery-specific and largely unpublished; where a number appears it is either a standardised
measure (`✓`) or a single-source trade figure (`~`). **A direction you can rely on and a recipe
you cannot is a better trade than a recipe that looks authoritative and is wrong.**

---

## Reading a spec: the four windows are four separate claims

Most leather descriptions collapse the windows into one adjective. They are independent, and a
spec should say which window it is describing:

```
MATERIAL   Bovine butt (animal/anatomy) · chrome-tanned (window 2: soft, water-resistant,
           Ts > 100 C, will not patina) · veg-retanned for body (window 3: firmer, fills) ·
           milled (window 3: softer, rounded grain) · semi-aniline (window 4: light pigment
           coat, medium stain resistance, still breathable)
```

That line is honest. "Quality leather, 1.4 mm" is not.

## What this folder refuses to do

- **It does not rank tanning methods as better or worse.** Chrome accounts for 80–90 % of world
  production ✓ for reasons of speed and cost, and vegetable tanning survives for reasons of
  character and tradition. Which is right depends on the object.
- **It does not quote vendor performance numbers as if they were measurements.** Where a number
  is a trade figure with no test method it is marked `~`. Where it is a standardised measure
  (shrinkage temperature) it is marked `✓`.
- **It does not pretend the four windows are the whole story.** Two whole branches sit outside
  them: **hair-on** (where the beamhouse does not unhair, `09`) and **not-leather** (materials
  sold alongside, `10`).
- **It does not describe avant-garde treatments as available.** Transparent leather is real but
  constrained; most chromic and conductive leathers are laboratory or pilot materials. The
  files say which.
