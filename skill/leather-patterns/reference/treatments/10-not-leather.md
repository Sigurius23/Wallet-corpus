# Not leather — the false friends

A compendium of leather treatments that omits the materials sold *as* leather is incomplete in
the way that matters most: **a designer who cannot tell these from leather will specify one and
pattern it as the other.**

None of these have collagen structure. None of them behave like leather at a fold, at a cut
edge, under a burnisher, or over ten years.

---

## The materials

| Material | What it actually is | Tells |
|---|---|---|
| **"Genuine leather"** | **not a grade — a marketing term.** Usually a real leather, most often a **heavily processed and coated split** ✓; the word certifies composition only, not quality | a real leather of unstated grade; often heavily coated |
| **Bonded leather** | leather **fibre and scrap** bonded with a binder (latex/PU) and rolled into a sheet ✓ | uniform, no grain structure, **frays at cut edges**, low strength, delaminates |
| **Leather board / leatherboard** | similar, compressed; used as **stiffener** inside goods, not as a face | a sheet good, structural not aesthetic |
| **PU leather / polyurethane** | a **PU coating on a fabric or a split** | a film on a substrate; fails by coating delamination |
| **PVC / vinyl** | plasticised PVC | heavier, colder, no breathability, plasticiser migration |
| **Microfibre leather ("microfibre", "ultrasuede")** | **microfibre nonwoven** with a PU elastomer, buffed to a nap ✓ | excellent and often better than leather for specific jobs — **but it is a nonwoven** |
| **Vegan leather (plant-based)** | plant waste or mycelium, usually **PU-coated** to be durable ✓ | see `07-avant-garde.md` — animal-free but often not plastic-free |
| **Recycled leather** | leather offcuts re-bonded | bonded leather by another name |

## The "genuine leather" trap — and the trap inside the trap

**"Genuine leather" is not a grade.** It has no regulated definition in any major market; it
generally means only that the material *contains* real hide, with the grade unstated ✓. In
practice it usually indicates **split leather that has been heavily processed and coated** —
often a lower split with an artificial grain applied ✓. But the term itself certifies nothing.

**The trap inside the trap is the ladder everyone repeats.** The familiar ranking —

> Full grain > Top grain > Genuine / Split > Bonded

— **is not an official grading scale.** No government and no leather trade body uses it ✓. It is
an internet convention. These are informal *market* terms describing what happened to the surface,
not a regulated hierarchy. Some excellent products are stamped "genuine leather"; some poor ones
say "full grain."

**What the terms do mean, descriptively:**

| Term | What it describes |
|---|---|
| **Full grain** | the outer surface intact — no sanding, no splitting away, no embossed grain ✓ |
| **Top grain** | the upper hide, **buffed or sanded** to remove surface variation, then finished. (Note: the trade often uses "top grain" to mean *any* non-split leather, including full grain ✓ — the word is not used consistently) |
| **Corrected grain** | the grain **sanded away** and an artificial grain applied ✓ |
| **Split** | the **flesh-side (corium)** layer, finished as a face; no natural grain ✓ |
| **Bonded** | reconstituted from fibre and scrap with a binder ✓ |

**The practical rule, and it is a real one:** never specify "genuine leather", "real leather" or
"premium leather" in a spec. Specify **full grain, corrected grain, or split-plus-coating-name** ✓.
An unqualified "leather" is the term regulators treat as deceptive unless the product is
substantially leather throughout.

**What regulation does say:** the FTC leather guides govern composition claims — if a product
contains bonded leather or a non-leather component, that must be disclosed, and bonded leather
must not be represented as leather ✓. The EU standard **EN 15987** sets a **50 % dry leather by
weight** minimum for the word "leather" ✓. Neither defines "genuine".

## What to ask instead of "is it real leather?"

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Full grain or split?"** | the actual grade question — the one word that carries information |
| **"Is the grain natural or embossed?"** | corrected grain is embossed; embossed grain is a printed film |
| **"What is the coating weight?"** | a very heavy coating turns leather into a coated fabric |
| **"Is there a flocked or laminated backing?"** | lamination is not leather |
| **"What happens at a cut edge — does it fray?"** | **the fastest field test for bonded leather.** Leather does not fray; bonded leather does ✓ |

## Why this matters for a pattern specifically

| Material | Pattern consequence |
|---|---|
| **Bonded leather** | **cannot be skived thin without failing**, frays at cut edges, will not burnish, **delaminates at flex** — do not use at a hinge |
| **PU / vinyl** | does not stretch the way leather does; **no grain to burnish**; edges are cut and painted or left; heat-sensitive |
| **Microfibre** | excellent dimensional stability and uniformity — **often easier to pattern than leather**; but it does not mould, does not patina, and its edges are not leather edges |
| **Coated split ("genuine")** | behaves like a thin leather with a film — the **film is the failure mode**, not the substrate |
| **Plant-based vegan** | unknown mechanics; **re-validate the pattern**, do not assume leather's stretch or grain behaviour ✓ |

**The honest position:** microfibre and the better plant-based materials are legitimate
engineering materials. The problem is never that they are not leather. The problem is that they
are specified *as* leather and then patterned with leather's assumptions — the fold allowance,
the burnished edge, the wet-form, the nap direction — none of which carry over automatically.
