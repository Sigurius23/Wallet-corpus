# Sources — treatments

**Part 3 of the source record.** Part 1 (§A–§D) and Part 2 (§E–§H) are in
`../anatomy/sources.md`; §I–§L cover the species work. This file continues the series for the
process layer.

**Reliability marks used throughout the folder:** `✓` = consistent across multiple independent
sources or a standardised measure · `~` = single-source, vendor, or compiled source — directional
only · no mark = general practice, reproducible in direction, unquantified.

## What could not be sourced, and stays open

1. **Any independent mechanical test of transparent leather.** No tensile, flex or abrasion data
   from a neutral party for any of the patent claims or the Ecco material. The folder says so
   explicitly rather than softening it.
2. **The Ecco process.** Trade secret. Not explained, anywhere.
3. **Quantitative beamhouse relationships.** "More lime → softer" is directional and consistent;
   the curve is not published.
4. **Retanning and fatliquor recipes.** Trade-specific, not public.
5. **Thermochromic cycle life.** Only marketing figures; no independent test.
6. **Cr(VI) formation conditions in finished leather.** The Cr(III)/Cr(VI) distinction is solid ✓;
   the conditions under which Cr(VI) can form in service are regulated but not cleanly summarised
   in any source found.
7. **A standard for "patina".** Does not exist.

## §R — Unhairing routes, compared

| Source | Contributed |
|---|---|
| **eajsti.org — "Assessment of the change in the conformation…" (four unhairing routes)** | the measured comparison, same stock, crust leather: **conventional 30.3 MPa / 40.1 N / 60.5 %; oxidative 21.8 / 34.1 / 39.6; hair-saving 32.1 / 46.4 / 64.2; painting 30.3 / 38.6 / 41.5** ✓~. **Oxidative unhairing failed the grain-crack standard**; mechanism proposed as pH-shift-driven isoelectric-point change plus collagen side-chain breaking. Also: **hair-saving produces a cleaner grain surface and simplifies dyeing** ✓ |
| **p2infohouse.org — "What is leather" (process notes)** | **sweating at 22–27 °C** until the wool pulls, used where **the wool is worth more than the skin** ✓; **paint unhairing recipe ~50 lime : 50 water : 5–20 sodium sulphide**, applied flesh-side, penetrating to dissolve the keratin round the hair root ✓; note that at **28–30 °C less swelling gives a finer, flatter grain** ✓; oxidative unhairing via chlorine dioxide |
| **degruyter / AUT review — "Use of enzymatic processes in the tanning of leather"** | **unhairing generates ~60–70 % of total process pollution** ✓; **oxidative unhairing cuts BOD/COD ~40 %** ✓; keratinase/protease routes; **"there is no enzymatic technology that does not damage the hides in the unhairing process"** ✓; enzyme production cost and the low K/C specificity ratio are the limits |
| **sltc.org — hair-saving enzyme-assisted unhairing** | mechanical properties **not impaired** vs normal production, **but the buffing effect on the grain is more noticeable in enzyme-unhaired leathers, and dye absorption is uneven** ✓ |
| **leathermag.com — hybrid extracts for light leathers** | **vegetable tannins have a negative effect on light and heat fastness and colour the crust, making dyeing harder** ✓; **tara and chestnut give the best heat and light fastness**; chestnut colours strongly; **tara is pale but carries insolubles/fibrous material** that cause application problems ✓ |

## §S — Which vegetable tannin, and why

| Source | Contributed |
|---|---|
| **tannins.org — "Choosing the right tannin for vegetable tanning and retanning"** (2026) | the cleanest modern statement of the selection logic: **chestnut = firmness + very good light fastness + abrasion; quebracho = fullness, softness, warm colour, ageing; tara = light colour, smooth grain, light and heat resistance; mimosa = versatility, fast penetration, good fullness** ✓. **"There is no single best vegetable tannin"** ✓; blending is normal and deliberate ✓ |
| **taninos.tripod.com — vegetable tanning (technical)** | **condensed (catechol) vs hydrolysable (pyrogallol)** classification; condensed **polymerise into phlobaphenes → "reds"**, suppressed by **sulphiting with 3–8 % sodium bisulphite at 98 °C** ✓; **chestnut is strongly astringent → slow penetration but irreversible, heavy fixation**, hence sole leather ✓; **sumac is the purest hydrolysable, gives yellow-hazel, full and even leather, excellent dye levelness with anionic and basic dyes** ✓; **tara contains practically no colouring matter, gives fullness + fine closed grain, and the highest grain resistance to breaking load of any vegetable tannin** ✓; **3 % chrome is enough to form stable bonds vs the vegetable equivalent** ✓ |
| **labretagna.com — "The Tannins"** | tannin-by-tannin colour and use; **chestnut as the main sole tannin** ✓; **mimosa lighter and pinker than quebracho, rapid penetration, high fixing, used to offset quebracho's red in sole leather** ✓ |
| **skillcult.com — tanning materials** | **sumac tannin is destroyed by boiling — extract at 50–60 °C** ✓; sumac yields an **almost white** leather and has a **bleaching/brightening** effect in retanning ✓; quebracho's low non-tannin content keeps leather "sweet" (does not generate the acids needed for plumping), so it is blended ✓ |

## §T — Hair-on, shearling, and the grade vocabulary

| Source | Contributed |
|---|---|
| **naturescollection.us / leatheroutlet.us / sheepskinleathers.com (converging)** | **shearling = sheepskin tanned with the wool on, then shorn to a level pile**, typically **~½–1 inch for garments**, shorter for boot lining, longer for rugs ✓; **all shearling is sheepskin, not all sheepskin is shearling** ✓; **the leather and wool are one piece — nothing glued or sewn**, which is why there is no gap between shell and insulation ✓; **in a garment the wool faces the body and the suede skin faces out as protection and structure** ✓; **cut-edge test: real shearling is continuous at the edge, faux shearling stops at a backing** ✓; faux is sold as **faux shearling / sherpa** |
| **fybagcustom.com — B2B sourcing decoder** | **"genuine leather" is not a quality grade — it is a legal minimum**, and in practice **coated split** ✓; the **#1 failure mode of coated split is coating delamination**, reported at **6–18 months** ✓~; the practical advice to **never write "genuine leather" in a spec** ✓ |
| **bespokewestern.com / grainmarkleather.com / Wikipedia-sourced summaries** | **"genuine leather" does not describe a specific grade** ✓; **the Full grain > Top grain > Genuine > Bonded ladder is not an official grading scale and no trade body uses it** ✓; **grade labels are informal market conventions, not regulated standards** ✓; **FTC 16 CFR Part 24** governs footwear composition claims and **does not use the phrase "genuine leather" at all** ✓; **bonded leather must not be represented as leather** ✓; **EN 15987:2011 sets a 50 % dry leather by weight minimum** for the word ✓ |

## What stays open after this pass

1. **The four-route unhairing comparison is a single study.** Direction is sound, magnitudes are
   not transferable.
2. **Tannin selection logic is well sourced; proportions are not published.**
3. **The shearling sources are retail and converge**, which makes them reliable on *definition*
   and unreliable on *performance*. Pile-length ranges are trade convention ~.
4. **"Genuine leather" has no definition to find**, by construction. The finding is the absence.
5. Still no source gives **goldenrod** as anything but anecdotal; the natural-dye fastness table
   remains craft-practice, flagged.
