# Hair-on, wool-on and shearling

**A leather family defined by a step it does not perform.** Everything else in this folder
starts with destroying or loosening the hair. Here, the hair *is the product*, and that one
decision changes the beamhouse, the tannage choice, the finish and the pattern.

Its own file because it is not a treatment you add — it is a different branch of the tree.

---

## The four members

| Product | Definition | Face | Back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hair-on** | hide tanned with the **hair intact** — calf, cow, goat, pony, springbok, antelope | the natural hair, in its own pattern and direction | usually a finished or sueded flesh side |
| **Wool-on / sheepskin (unsheared)** | sheepskin tanned with the **fleece at its natural length**, not levelled — the rug and throw market | long wool ✓ | the skin |
| **Shearling** | sheepskin **tanned with the wool on, then shorn to a level pile** ✓ — the "shearling" most people mean | a cut pile, **typically ~½–1 inch for garments** ✓, shorter for boot linings, longer for rugs | sueded or finished skin |
| **Double face** | a sheepskin where **both sides are used as face**: fleece on one side, skin on the other ✓ | fleece | sueded/napped skin |

**The distinction that matters: sheepskin is the broad word, shearling is the shorn one.** All
shearling is sheepskin; **not all sheepskin is shearling** ✓. The name comes from the levelling
step, and the difference is really one of *intent* — sheepskin keeps its own character and its
organic edge; shearling is the same material shorn level and cut into panels for a garment ✓.

> **Two things commonly got wrong, worth stating because they show up in every product page:**
> (1) **the wool is not glued or sewn in** — it is the same piece of hide, which is exactly why
> there is no gap between shell and insulation ✓; (2) **"shearling" does not mean the wool was
> sheared off and processed separately** — the wool stays on the skin and the *pile height* is
> adjusted. And the word for the fake is **faux shearling / sherpa**, which is a pile fabric on a
> separate backing — **the cut edge tells you:** real shearling is continuous, faux stops at a
> backing ✓.

**"Shearling" is still ambiguous in retail use** — some sellers use it for any wool-on sheepskin
whether or not the pile was levelled. Ask for the pile depth in millimetres rather than trusting
the noun.

## Why it needs its own beamhouse

| Standard step | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Unhairing** | **not performed as hair destruction.** Either the hair is saved (paint/paste unhairing on the flesh side, or no unhairing at all), or — for shearling — the wool is **shorn** as the product ✓ |
| **Liming** | must not attack the follicle or the wool root, or the fleece pulls |
| **Degreasing** | sheepskin is fat-rich; **but over-degreasing starves the wool** and makes it dry and brittle. A balance, not a maximum |
| **Bating** | restrained — heavy bating loosens the follicle and the hair sheds ✓ |
| **Splitting** | the skin is split **thin**, because the load is the fleece, not the skin |
| **Fatliquoring** | **less on the wool side, more on the skin side** — the two faces want opposite treatments |

**The consequence:** hair-on and shearling leathers are usually **thin, soft and weak in the
skin**, because the skin is a carrier for the hair, not the structure. Do not expect a hair-on
calf to have the tensile of a veg-tan butt of the same thickness.

**In a garment the skin is the *shell*, not a carrier.** On a shearling jacket the wool faces the
body as insulation and the sueded skin faces out as the protection and structure ✓ — so the skin
does have a structural role. It is simply a **thin, suede-faced** structure, which is a poor
abrasion surface. That is the real constraint, not "the skin does not matter".

## The pattern problems specific to hair-on

### 1. Nap direction is not a preference, it is a constraint

The hair lies in a direction. **A hair-on piece has a definite "with" and "against",** and:

- **Pieces cut in opposite directions look like different colours** under light — the same hide
  can read as two shades.
- **The pile crushes and does not recover.** A fold pressed into hair-on leaves a mark that
  cannot be steamed out the way a wool garment can.
- **Sewing pulls hair into the seam.** A seam allowance on hair-on must be **clipped clear of the
  hair**, or the seam is bulky and the hair is caught in the stitch line.
- **Cut edges shed.** Hair-on cut edges are untidy and shed continuously; they need binding,
  folding under, or a deliberate raw-edge aesthetic.

### 2. Direction of cut is a real, visible spec

```
GRAIN  all hair-on panels cut NAP-DOWN (or NAP-UP — state which), single direction only
```
**Never** lay hair-on pieces opposing each other and call the result a colour match. It will
not be.

### 3. Thickness and structure

Hair-on hides are usually **0.8–1.4 mm of skin plus the pile** ✓~. So:

- The **skin** behaves like a thin, soft, chrome-tanned leather.
- The **pile** adds apparent thickness and compressibility but **no structural strength**.
- **A hair-on hinge is a thin-soft-leather hinge.** Apply the flex rules in `../durability.md`
  to the skin thickness alone, not to the total.

### 4. Which species, and what that means

| Animal | Hair character | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Calf** | short, fine, even | the classic hair-on calf; bags, small goods, upholstery |
| **Cow / steer** | longer, coarser, more variation | rugs, larger goods |
| **Goat** | short, flat, lies tight | small goods; **pattern is directional and visible** |
| **Pony / horse** | short, sleek | specialist |
| **Springbok / antelope** | short, dense, sharply patterned | rugs, decorative |
| **Sheep (shearling)** | wool; crimped | garments, linings, boot linings, collars |
| **Pig** | bristle — **not usable as a garment hair-on**; the commercial pigskin is dehaired | — |

### 5. Shearling specifically

- **The pile has a natural crimp and a definite nap.** Cut all pieces the same way.
- **Pile length is the spec number** — typically quoted in millimetres, and it decides the
  garment's warmth and its bulk at seams.
- **Seams in shearling must be butted or skived clear of the pile.** A standard seam with a
  10 mm allowance and a full pile gives a seam twice the thickness of the panel.
- **Shearling cannot be edge-painted.** Edges are folded, bound, or left as a cut pile edge.
- **Shearling is a poor choice for any high-flex, high-abrasion area** — the pile abrades and
  matts; the skin beneath is thin.

## What changes in the property matrix

| Property | Hair-on / shearling vs the same skin dehaired |
|---|---|
| **Thickness** | apparent ↑↑ (pile), structural — |
| **Strength** | skin is **thinner and weaker** — the fibre structure was never worked for strength |
| **Stretch** | skin ↑ soft and stretchy; **pile direction adds a second anisotropy** |
| **Abrasion** | pile mats and abrades; **not a wear surface** |
| **Water** | **↓↓** — the pile wicks and holds water; the skin beneath usually has minimal finish |
| **Insulation** | **↑↑** — the reason it exists |
| **Repairability** | **↓↓** — crushed pile, shed hair and cut edges are not repairable |
| **Cutting** | **special** — nap direction, pile clearance, shedding |
| **Cost** | ↑↑ — the animal has to be good enough that its hair is worth keeping |

## Specifying it

```
MATERIAL  hair-on calf, 1.2 mm skin + 6 mm hair, chrome-tanned
NAP       all panels cut NAP-DOWN, single direction
EDGES     bound [or] folded under — cut edges shed
SEAMS     allowance clipped clear of hair; butted [or] skived
AVOID     high-flex hinges, abrasion faces, wet use
MATERIAL  shearling, 18 mm pile, garment weight
SEAMS     pile clipped from allowances; butted seams
FINISH    no edge paint — bind or fold
```

## Honest limits

- **The category split and the beamhouse differences are solid** ✓ — they follow necessarily
  from not destroying hair.
- **Pile-length figures and skin thicknesses are trade ranges** ~; they vary by tannery and by
  animal.
- **"Hair-on" and "wool-on" are used loosely in retail**; not every "shearling" jacket uses
  skin-on-fleece, and some "shearling" linings are synthetic. Ask.
- **Hair-on tanning chemistry is not published** in comparable detail — it is a smaller, more
  artisanal part of the industry.
