# Window 3 — post-tanning

**"It is not possible to build the exact properties required into the leathers during the tanning
operation"** ✓ (Leather Naturally, *Making Leather* Part 3). Tanning stabilises; **post-tanning is
where the character is built.**

This window contains the steps that decide temper, colour, break and area yield. It is also
where most of the zero-sum trade-offs live.

---

## The sequence

| # | Step | What it does | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Samming / setting** | mechanical water removal, sets the grain | — |
| 2 | **Splitting / shaving** | band knife splits; **shaving** takes the leather to final thickness with spiral knives | sets the uniform thickness; **shaving can expose the grain–corium junction** — see the skiving paradox below |
| 3 | **Neutralisation** | **raising the pH to ~4.5–5.5** with mild alkali (bicarbonate, acetate) ✓ | **controls depth of penetration of everything that follows.** A deeper neutralisation lets dye, retan and fatliquor go deeper |
| 4 | **Retanning** | vegetable tannins, syntans, acrylic resins, fillers ✓ | **firmness or softness, fullness, grain fineness, dyeability, lightfastness** |
| 5 | **Dyeing** | drum (through-dyed) or surface (spray/pad) | colour depth and where the colour sits — see `04-dyes.md` |
| 6 | **Fatliquoring** | emulsified oils worked into the fibre, ~60–66 °C for 30–40 min ✓ | **softness, pliability, lubricity** — the step that stops leather drying board-stiff |
| 7 | **Fixation** | acidification (formic acid) or fixative to exhaust the drum | locks dye and fatliquor so they do not migrate |
| 8 | **Drying** | vacuum, toggle, paste, hang, tunnel | **area yield and stretch.** Drying under tension → firmer, less stretch; drying free → softer, more area |
| 9 | **Conditioning** | water added back to **18–28 %** ✓ | prepares for mechanical softening; too dry → cracking in staking |
| 10 | **Staking** | mechanical flexing to break the bonds formed on drying ✓ | softness |
| 11 | **Milling / dry drumming** | tumbling dry in a drum for hours ✓ | softness + **rounds and deepens the grain** (the "milled grain" look) |
| 12 | **Buffing** | sanding — grain side = **nubuck**, flesh side = **suede** ✓ | nap; removes the outer protective layer |
| 13 | **Finishing** | see `05-finishing.md` | — |

---

## The four levers, and what each costs

### 1. Retanning

Materials applied to modify the structure ✓: **vegetable extracts, syntans, acrylic resins,
fillers.** In practice several are used together.

| You want | Add | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Firmer, denser, more body | more vegetable extract in the retan ✓ | less flexibility, darker colour, more water sensitivity |
| Softer, fuller | resins, fillers, syntans | can feel "plastic", reduces natural grain character |
| Better dye uptake and levelness | selected syntans (e.g. phenol-sulphonic condensates) ✓ | — |
| Fill the grain and grain–corium junction | selective filling agents ✓ | over-filling → coated, artificial hand |

**Retanning is the step where a chrome tannery makes leather stop feeling like chrome leather.**
It is also where "chrome-free" claims get complicated: a chrome-tanned hide with a heavy
vegetable retan is sold in some markets as a vegetable-tanned product.

### 2. Fatliquoring — the softness lever, with a real cost

A fatliquor is **oil chemically treated so it emulsifies with water, penetrates, and lubricates
the fibre** ✓; the emulsion penetrates and then splits, depositing oil in the structure ✓.

> **The trade-off, in the industry's own words:** *"the deeper this penetration and the greater
> the offer, the softer the leather, but the greater the tendency to develop a coarse break"* ✓.

**So softness and grain tightness trade directly against each other.** A glove-soft leather has
a loose break by construction. If you want a leather that both drapes and holds a crisp
burnished edge, you are asking for opposite ends of one lever.

Other fatliquor facts worth knowing:

- **Source matters**: synthetic, fish, vegetable, animal, tallow, even greases ✓.
- **Always after dyeing** — the oils would otherwise interfere with dye uptake ✓.
- **Wet-white and aldehyde leathers need *more* fatliquor than chrome at the same thickness** ✓
  because the aldehyde reaction consumes the amino groups that dye and fatliquor would use.
- **Less is needed for vegetable-tanned leathers** at the same thickness ✓ — the tannin matrix
  is already filled and only needs surface lubrication.

### 3. Drying — the area-versus-stability lever

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| **Toggle / nail drying** (stretched on frames) | **more area, tighter, less stretch** — the leather is held flat while it dries |
| **Vacuum drying** | flat, firm, fast; used for consistent substance |
| **Paste drying** (glued to glass/metal) | very flat and firm, high yield, minimal stretch |
| **Hanging / suspension** | softer, more natural, less area |

**This is where "area yield" comes from.** Two hides of the same weight can measure very
differently in square feet because one was toggled and one hung. The toggled one is bigger and
flatter and has less stretch — which for a pattern maker is usually the better trade.

### 4. Mechanical softening — staking and milling

- **Staking** flexes the leather repeatedly to break the bonds that formed as it dried ✓.
  Under-staked leather is stiff; over-staked can thin and stretch the grain.
- **Milling / dry drumming** tumbles it for hours ✓ — softens **and rounds the grain**, which is
  the origin of the "milled" or "tumbled" look.
- **Dry milling** is specifically described as *"tumbled rapidly in a drum under dry conditions
  to soften it and make the natural grain pattern more evident"* ✓.

## The skiving paradox, in process terms

`../durability.md` records that a skive can be unsafe because it exposes the grain–corium
junction. The post-tanning view of the same fact:

**Shaving is the same operation as skiving, done at the tannery, at scale, on the flesh side.**
It is what makes leather uniform in millimetres. And it is also what can bring the weaker
reticular corium close to the surface, or (if overdone) remove the densest part of the grain
layer entirely.

Practical consequences:

- **The quoted thickness is a decision, not a property of the animal.** A 0.8 mm and a 1.4 mm
  version of the same article may be the same hide at different shaving settings.
- **Shaving more does not make leather softer proportionally** — it makes it *thinner*, and
  below a point it makes it *weaker*, because you are cutting into the structural corium.
- **A maker who skives a strap thinner to make it bend** is doing the same thing to the same
  layer, with less skill and no thickness gauge. See the skiving rule in `../durability.md`.

---

## Buffing — and the direction it removes protection

| Buffed on | Product | What is removed |
|---|---|---|
| **grain side** | **nubuck** | the tight outer grain surface, replaced by a fine nap of the grain layer |
| **flesh side** | **suede** | nothing protective — the flesh split becomes the face |

**Both remove the most durable part of the leather and replace it with a high-surface-area nap
that absorbs liquid.** That is why nubuck and suede stain where a full-grain does not, why
nubuck is *slightly* more durable than suede (finer collagen fibres in the grain layer than in
the corium ✓), and why neither should be specified for a wear face that will see abrasion and
water.

---

## What post-tanning cannot do

- **Fix a loose beamhouse.** No amount of retanning tightens an over-bated grain.
- **Make leather both very soft and very crisp.** The fatliquor lever decides.
- **Give chrome leather a vegetable patina.** The tannins are not there.
- **Restore thickness.** Shaving only removes.

## Honest limits

- Neutralisation pH ranges, fatliquor temperatures and conditioning moisture are **consistent
  across tannery process documentation** ✓ (UNIC, Cortina, Leather Naturally, agriculture.institute).
- **Retanning recipes are trade-specific and not published.** The material classes and their
  effects are reliable; quantities are not given here because they are not public.
- The softness-vs-break quote is from Leather Naturally's own training material ✓ and is the
  clearest statement of a trade-off that the whole industry manages silently.

# Part 2 — Repairability: which treatments are reversible

**A pattern maker's second question about any treatment is not "what does it do" but "can it be
undone?"** Retanning, dyeing and finishing all sit in a hierarchy of permanence, and a design
that assumes reversibility where there is none is a design that cannot be corrected.

| Treatment | Reversible? | How, or why not |
|---|---|---|
| **Soaking / re-wetting** | **yes** | leather can be re-wetted and re-shaped (especially veg-tan) |
| **Conditioning / oiling** | **yes** | add oil; the classic remedy for dried leather |
| **Fatliquor level** | **partly** | more can be added; **excess cannot be removed** |
| **Staking / milling (softness)** | **partly** | more milling softens further; **softness cannot be taken out** |
| **Shaving / skiving** | **no** | material removed is gone |
| **Retanning** | **no** | can be layered, not undone; a veg retan permanently changes the leather |
| **Dyeing — drum** | **no** | can only be dyed darker or over-dyed; never lighter |
| **Dyeing — surface** | **partly** | can be cleaned back somewhat; not reliably |
| **Iron mordant** | **no** | and it continues to act — see `04-dyes.md` |
| **Pigmented finish** | **partly** | can be stripped and re-finished professionally; not by a user |
| **Patent / heavy lacquer** | **no** | once it cracks it cannot be repaired invisibly |
| **Edge paint** | **yes** | can be stripped and re-applied — a genuine advantage over burnishing |
| **Burnished edge** | **yes** | re-sand and re-burnish ✓ |
| **Emboss / engrave** | **no** | the relief is set |
| **Perforation** | **no** | holes are permanent |
| **Wet-formed shape** | **partly** | can be re-wetted and re-formed, with some loss |
| **Pleating / heat set** | **no** | the crease is plastic deformation |
| **Transparent leather treatment** | **no** | the filler is in the bulk |
| **Flame retardant / antimicrobial finish** | **no** | in the fibre or bonded to it |

## The pattern-level rules this generates

1. **Softness is a one-way door.** If you mill a leather soft, you cannot mill it firm. Specify
   the soft end deliberately; a supplier can always soften more, never less.
2. **Colour is a one-way door downwards.** Drum dyeing to black is final. Plan the colour before
   the leather is dyed, not after the object is made.
3. **Burnishing beats edge paint on repairability *and* durability — but only on veg-tan.** On
   chrome-tan, edge paint is the only option and it is a maintenance item. Say which you are
   specifying and why.
4. **A finished panel is a committed panel.** Retan, finish and emboss are all irreversible. If
   the design may need to change, keep the leather un-finished until the design is settled.

## The one thing that is more reversible than people think

**Vegetable-tanned leather.** It can be re-wetted, re-shaped, re-burnished, re-dyed darker, and
in conservation it can be partially de-tanned and re-treated. This is a large part of why it is
the traditional material for anything that must be made, adjusted and repaired by hand.

**Chrome-tanned leather is more stable but less workable.** It will not re-form, will not
re-burnish, and will not take a patina. Its advantage is that it also does not need to.
