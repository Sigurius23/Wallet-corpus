# Plate 02 — text version: what is hard inside the skin (L3.5)

**The plate is in four horizontal sections:** six surface-texture swatches; the ostrich
"one bird, four materials" figure with a foot detail; four failure-mode diagrams; and the
replacement rule for the fold-strain screen. This document is the plate in words.

The plate's subtitle states its argument: **the pattern *is* the mechanics — a soft dome, a
hinged plate and a fused bead each fail differently.**

---

## Section 1 — Where the hard part sits (six swatches)

Each swatch is a small filled tile showing the surface texture, with a name and two lines
beneath it.

| # | Swatch texture | Name | Line 1 | Line 2 (the mechanical verdict) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | rounded dots on a warm brown field | **OSTRICH QUILL** | empty follicle, raised collar | **SOFT · creases like calf** |
| 2 | overlapping shingled arcs on grey | **SNAKE SCALE** | a fold of skin, free edge | **FAILS BY LIFTING** |
| 3 | large rounded rectangles in a column | **OSTRICH LEG SCUTE** | large plates, front column | **HARD · hinges between** |
| 4 | dense pale rounded beads on dark | **STINGRAY DENTICLE** | enameloid on dentin, fused | **HARD · never peels** |
| 5 | small sharp pale dots in rows | **SHARK DENTICLE** | small, sharp — usually | **GROUND OFF · normal leather** |
| 6 | a raised ridge over a banded stack | **CROCODILIAN OSTEODERM** | bone under the dorsal scales | **BRITTLE · caiman creases** |

**The point of putting them side by side:** three of these are hard, one is soft, one is
hard-but-removed, and one is bone. "Exotic leather has scales" is a useless sentence. The
distinction that predicts behaviour is **what the hard part is made of and whether it is
attached**.

## Section 2 — One bird, four materials (ostrich)

### The body figure

An oval body outline in pale neutral. Inside it, a rotated square (diamond) of quill texture,
labelled at its centre:

> **CROWN** · **1/3 of skin**

Beneath the body:

> **FULL QUILL** — crown, central back — soft, oil-rich
>
> **SMOOTH** — same hide, off the crown — no follicles

**The geometric fact: the diamond is asked for by name.** The crown is diamond-shaped, and
the drawing shows it as a rotated square inside an oval body — i.e. the most valuable third of
a skin sits in the middle of it, and the corners of the hide cannot be used for the same
product.

### The leg figure

A narrow vertical strip, split into three vertical columns by fill:

- **centre column** — large scute texture
- **left and right columns** — small scale texture, slightly washed out
- a dark cap band across the top
- two pale circles at the bottom, side by side

Beneath the leg:

> **LEG SKIN** — scutes front · scales sides — soft folds at back — **toughest cut,
> sharpest edge**

### The foot detail box

A bordered box titled **THE FOOT — didactyl, two toes**, containing:

- a large upright toe shape in scute texture, ending in a solid pale rounded nail
- beside it a much shorter toe shape in scale texture, ending in a **dashed** outline

Labelled:

> **toe 1: wide** · **HOOF-LIKE NAIL**
> **toe 2:** · **no claw**
> *(below the box)* **sole = dense keratinised pads**

**The dashed end on toe 2 is the whole point of the box.** One toe ends in a hard, broad nail;
the other ends in nothing. A leg skin therefore has **one** rigid terminus, not two — which is
why the "check which end the seller cut" note appears in the callout.

### The callout box — "Why the foot matters to a pattern"

Reproduced verbatim:

> - Toe pads: dense, keratinised — abrasion-resistant, terrible at deforming
> - The nail is a RIGID INCLUSION at one end: you cannot fold, skive or stitch near it
> - Two toes ⟹ two claw ends per leg skin — check which end the seller cut
> - The leg has its own front/back map: scutes that resist folding at the front, loose folds
>   at the back
>
> **Use feet for edges, facings, decorative termini — never for a hinge.**

*(Note: the callout says "two claw ends" while the foot diagram shows one toe with a nail and
one without a claw. The anatomically careful statement is **two toes per foot, of which only
one carries a broad hoof-like nail** — and by extension a leg skin has one hard terminus per
toe, so check which. The diagram is right; the callout line is loose and should be read as
"check the ends".)*

## Section 3 — Four surface types, four failure modes

Four boxes, each with a title, a small diagram, and a one-line consequence. The diagrams are
the argument; here is what each shows.

| Box | Title | What the diagram shows | Caption |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SOFT CONTINUOUS (leather)** | a solid bar; underneath, a single smooth curve bending away and back | **strain spreads · crack must initiate** |
| 2 | **PLATED  croc / stingray / leg** | a row of separated plates above; underneath, a **zigzag** line that steps at every gap | **hinges at every gap · strain localises** |
| 3 | **FOLD-SCALED  snake / lizard** | a row of overlapping arcs; two short curved red strokes lifting off the arcs | **free edges peel · fix is ADHESION** |
| 4 | **FUSED BEAD (stingray)** | a row of touching circles; underneath one straight line with a small arc beneath it | **nothing peels · the matrix takes the strain** |

**Reading the four together is the lesson.** Box 1 is the assumption every formula in
`../durability.md` rests on. Box 2 says the strain path is not smooth — it is a staircase, and
all the deformation lands in the gaps. Box 3 says the failure is not a crack at all, it is
adhesion. Box 4 is the only one of the four where the hard parts cannot come off, so the
failure moves into the soft phase.

## Section 4 — The rule that replaces the fold-strain screen

Reproduced verbatim:

> For a plated or scaled hide, do not ask "what is the outer-fibre strain?". Ask:
> **does the fold line fall between two plates, or through one?**
>
> Between two plates — the hinge is the matrix, and it may last. Through a plate — the plate
> is what fails, and no radius will save it.
>
> *Crocodilian belly is the exception: low calcium, flat, regular scales — it can be used
> structurally. Caiman, with its osteoderms, cannot.*

## What this plate deliberately cannot show

- **Scale, in any absolute sense.** The swatches are texture cues, not micrographs. A quill is
  drawn at 13 px pitch and an osteoderm at 16 px; that ratio means nothing.
- **The soft phase between hard parts.** In every real plated hide the matrix is most of the
  area and most of the behaviour, and it is nearly invisible in a schematic.
- **Direction of the hard parts.** All rows on the plate run left-to-right. On a real leg, a
  snake, or a crocodile's back, the rows run along the body, and cutting across them changes
  the hinge pitch — see `appendages.md`.
