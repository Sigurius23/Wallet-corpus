# Plate 01 — text version: hide anatomy, where the material changes

**The plate shows two schematic animal outlines side by side, plus two through-thickness
cross-sections underneath.** It is a schematic: **the outlines are idealised and not to
scale; the boundaries are anatomical, the drawings are not.** Red dashed lines on the plate
are the trade's cut lines. This document is the plate in words.

---

## 1. The bovine outline — regions (L1) and cuts (L2)

The bovine figure is divided by four red dashed lines and five shaded bands, reading from
the front of the animal to the rear.

### The four cut lines

| Line | Where it runs | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Backbone** | vertical, down the centre, from head to tail | splits the hide into two **sides** |
| **Break of the fore flank** | horizontal, at the change of texture between shoulder and body | where a **shoulder** is cut off, at right angles to the backbone |
| **Belly line** | horizontal, below the back | above it the hide is firm, below it loose. An aged/sex-dependent line for grading |
| **Bend/butt line** | horizontal, above the rump | separates **bend** from **butt** |

### The five bands, front to back

| Band | Label on the plate | Sub-label | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **HEAD · CHEEK · FACE** | offal — thin, small, marked | discarded or sold as offal; small parts |
| 2 | **NECK · SHOULDER** | neck most mobile, most wrinkle · shoulder thick, firm · fore shanks at corners | the most mobile region, hence the most creasing; shoulder is thick and firm and takes tooling; the fore shanks sit at the outer corners |
| 3 | **BEND** (centre) | densest · most uniform · lowest stretch | the prime flat-panel region |
| 3 | **BELLY · MIDDLE** (both outer margins) | *(no sub-label)* | loose, stretchy, thin, defect-prone. Labelled vertically on each side |
| 4 | **BUTT** | thickest · hind shanks at corners | thickest, densest; hind shanks at the outer corners |
| 5 | **TAIL END** | *(no sub-label)* | the rearmost edge; lowest strength in location surveys |

### The five captions under the bovine figure

1. **backbone cut ⟶ two SIDES**
2. **belly line separates firm from loose**
3. **shoulder off at the break ⟶ DOUBLE SHOULDER · shoulder off a back ⟶ BEND**
4. **bellies + shoulder off ⟶ BUTT · untanned butt/bend ⟶ CROUPON**
5. *(in red)* **CULATTA (bovine) = butt + belly middles + hind shanks — a mixed cut**

### What the plate encodes but does not spell out

The whole figure is a **map, not a sheet**. Three axes are visible at once: position (bands),
the cut lines that turn positions into saleable products, and the fact that the bends and
belly margins are *the same hide* at different depth of weave. The bovine caption's real
content is the fourth line: **every L2 cut name is defined by where those four red lines
fall**, and tanneries do not all put them in the same place.

## 2. The equine outline — front, butt, and the layer inside the butt

The equine figure is divided by **one** red dashed line into three shaded zones.

| Zone | Label | Sub-labels |
|---|---|---|
| Upper | **FRONT — FQHH / DHF** | 1.0–1.2 mm · ~2/3 of hide area · thin, coarse grain, flexible |
| Middle (cross-hatched) | **STRIP — a.k.a. "horsebutt"** | 1.6–4 mm · very coarse grain |
| Lower | *(no label on the band itself)* | contains the two shells |

### The two shells

Drawn as two pale ovals side by side in the lower (rump) zone, each labelled:

> **SHELL** · **~1–2.5 ft²**

### The cut line and the captions

- The red dashed line carries the label **460 mm cut**.
- Caption 1: **cut 460 mm (18 in) forward of the tail root, at right angles to the backbone**
- Caption 2: **"horse butt" is a misnomer — the strip sits in front of the butt**
- Caption 3: **hyaline layer: equine rump only · no grain · rolls, never creases**
- Caption 4 *(red)*: **CULATTA (equine) = butt leather with shell traces on the flesh side**

### What the equine figure is really showing

That **the equine trade identifies a layer, not just regions.** The strip and the shells are
not "areas of the butt" — they are different depths of the same area, and the shell is worth
more than everything around it. That is the single illustration that makes L1/L2 versus L3
obvious.

## 3. Through the thickness (L3) — two cross-sections

Two stacks drawn side by side, top surface at the top of each stack.

### Bovine, generic — reading down

| Order | Layer | Label beside the stack |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | thin pale band | **epidermis 0.1–0.2 mm — removed** |
| 2 | mid-toned band | **GRAIN (papillary)** · carries the pore pattern |
| 3 | thin red line | **grain–corium junction** · where a finish lifts |
| 4 | dark thick band | **CORIUM (reticular) 80–90 %** |
| 5 | pale bottom band | **flesh split — carries the thickness variation** |

### Equine, rump only — reading down

| Order | Layer | Label beside the stack |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | thin pale band | **epidermis** |
| 2 | pale gold band | **HYALINE / SHELL** · no grain · ~10× denser |
| 3 | mid-toned band | *(the corium begins)* |
| 4 | thin red line | **grain–corium junction** |
| 5 | dark thick band | **CORIUM** |
| 6 | pale bottom band | **flesh split** · carries shell traces in a culatta |

**Note the structural difference the plate is making:** in the equine stack the **hyaline
layer sits above the grain–corium junction**, between epidermis and corium. That is the whole
reason cordovan behaves differently — it is not the grain side, and it is not the corium.
It is a third material in the stack.

### The two footnotes under the cross-sections

> A properly split and shaved leather can be near-uniform in **millimetres** while remaining
> very non-uniform in **weave and stretch**. Buy the thickness; respect the structure.
>
> **Full grain is a statement about the layer (L3), not about which part of the animal you
> got (L1).**

## What this plate deliberately cannot show

- **Real proportions.** The outlines are idealised. A real hide for a given animal is not
  this shape, and the region boundaries are not straight lines.
- **Thickness variation.** The cross-sections are drawn at uniform thickness because that is
  what a **split** leather is. Unsplit hide is wildly variable — see `properties.md` §3.
- **Where defects live.** Grading marks are a separate layer of information; a clean diagram
  implies a clean hide.
- **Species beyond bovine and equine.** See `plate-02.md` and `species/`.
