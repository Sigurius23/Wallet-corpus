# The taxonomy

Terms sorted by level. **Every entry states which level it belongs to**, because the
trade uses all five interchangeably and that is where the confusion starts.

Confidence key: **✓** sourced to a named source in `sources.md` · **~** single source
or trader blog, direction reliable, exact wording not normative · **✗** conflicting
definitions found, do not use without a diagram.

---

## L0 — Animal

| Term | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **hide** | skin of a **large** animal — cattle, horse, buffalo, pig | trade convention, not biology. Thick and heavy |
| **skin** | skin of a **small** animal — goat, sheep, calf, piglet | same organ, different processing economics |
| **kip** | skin of a **young bovine** (between calf and cow), or of certain Asiatic cattle | ~ ambiguous, avoid in a spec |
| **calf / baby calf** | young bovine, fine grain, thin | the reference material for "fine break" |
| **yearling** | 1–2 y bovine, between calf and cow | ~ mostly auction/raw-hide vocabulary |

Age drives **grain fineness and thickness**; sex drives **neck and shoulder character**
(a bull hide has a thick, rough head/neck/shoulder and coarse flanks ✓ — this is why ox
and cow hides differ in yield, not just size).

## L1 — Body regions (bovine)

The standard diagram (from the leather chemists' dictionary ✓) numbers nine:

| # | Region | Character |
|---|---|---|
| a | **cheek** | offal; thin, small, marked |
| b | **face** | offal |
| c | **shoulder** | thick, firm, wrinkle at the neck; good forming and tooling |
| d | **fore shank** | leg; dense, small, strongly curved |
| e | **belly** | loose, stretchy, thin, variable, most defect-prone |
| f | **bend** | back/flank quadrant; dense, but see the strength caveat |
| g | **butt** | rump; **thickest and densest**, least stretch, most uniform |
| h | **belly middle** | the mid-belly strip between fore and hind leg |
| i | **hind shank** | leg; dense, small |

Extended vocabulary adds: **neck** (above shoulder, most mobile → most wrinkle),
**flank** (between the last rib and the hip), **back / backbone line** (the strip either
side of the spine), **root of tail / tailend**.

**Definition anchors that are actually normative ✓.** Two "breaks" (change-of-texture
lines) define the cuts:

- The **break of the fore flank** — where the shoulder's firm weave turns loose.
  The shoulder is cut off at right angles to the backbone *at this line*.
- The **break of the hind flank**.

Above the **belly line**, the hide is firm; below it, loose. These are lines you can
find by hand on a real hide — they are not arbitrary.

## L2 — Commercial cuts (bovine)

This is the level that gets quoted as if it were a material spec. It is not: it is a
**vendor's outline**.

### The main cuts

| Cut | Where it comes from | Level-3 composition | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **whole hide** | everything | all regions, all layers | ✓ |
| **side** | whole hide split **down the backbone** | half of every region. Note the classic definition: a side is half a hide *with the offal — head, shoulder and belly — attached* | ✓ |
| **back** | hide split along backbone, head and belly trimmed off | bend + shoulder | ✓ |
| **crop** | a **side** with the belly trimmed off, **retaining head and shoulder** | — | ✓ |
| **double shoulder** | forepart cut at right angles to the backbone at the break of the fore flank, **belly off, head off behind the horn holes** | shoulder + neck | ✓ |
| **bend** | a **back with the shoulder cut off** at right angles to the backbone at the break of the fore flank | bend proper. "Bend" is used of **tanned** leather; the untanned equivalent is **croupon** | ✓ |
| **double bend** | both bends | — | ✓ |
| **butt** | hide left after **bellies and shoulder removed** | bend + butt | ✓ |
| **double butt** | both butts | — | ✓ |
| **belly** | below the belly line, underside and upper legs | — | ✓ |
| **belly middle** | mid-belly strip | — | ✓ |
| **culatta** | ✗ **conflict — see below** | — | ✗ |
| **offal** | heads, shoulders, bellies, shanks — everything that is **not** a bend | not waste, just lower grade | ✓ |
| **croupon** | untanned whole hide with belly cut off; untanned equivalent of a butt bend | — | ✓ |

### Cut → strength, as the trade grades it

Unofficial but consistent across sources ~: **bend = best, butt & shoulder = better,
belly = fair.** Treat this as a *market* ranking (uniformity, surface, workability),
not a tensile ranking — the tensile ranking is contested. See `properties.md`.

### The culatta conflict — read this before buying "culatta"

The same word names two different things, and only one of them is what most small-goods
makers think.

| Source | Definition of *culatta* |
|---|---|
| Classic leather-trade terminology ✓ | "the **rear part of a bovine hide**, comprising the **butt, the belly middles and the hind shanks**" — i.e. a *mixed* cut, deliberately fusing firm and loose leather |
| Equine / Italian artisanal usage ✓ | **horse butt with shell-cordovan traces on the flesh side** (see `horse.md`) |
| One retailer ~ | "rear section incorporating the bend and adjoining butt" |
| One retailer ~ | "the area from around the lower back and rear... **smoother and softer**" |
| One fashion glossary ~ | "usually horse butts with traces of shell cordovan on the reverse side" |

**These are not reconciliable.** The bovine definition is the one in the standard
terminology list and it explicitly *includes belly middles and hind shanks*. The
equine one is a different animal entirely.

> **Rule: never specify "culatta" without an attached diagram and a species.** For a
> bovine cut, say **"culatta: butt + belly middles + hind shanks"** and confirm the
> outline. For horse, say **"horse culatta (butt with shell traces)"**. If a supplier
> cannot send a diagram, the word is doing no work.

The bovine culatta is also *interesting as a material* rather than a bug: it is the one
commercial cut that deliberately spans two very different weaves in one piece, which is
why it turns up in artisanal footwear where the texture change is the point ~.

### Related cut words you will meet

**Analine / aniline** — dye, irrelevant to anatomy. **Strip** — in the **bovine** trade
a *strip* is a **half-hide** (half neck + half butt + belly) ✓; in the **equine** trade
a *strip* is the narrow band **just in front of the butt**, above the shells ✓. Another
cross-species collision — see `horse.md`.

## L3 — Layers (the through-thickness axis)

Uniform across hoofed mammals except where noted.

| Layer | Fraction | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **epidermis** | 0.1–0.2 mm on bovine ✓ | removed in leather making; only the hair follicle holes remain, and they *are* the grain |
| **grain layer / papillary dermis** | goat 24–54 % of thickness, varying butt 24–40 %, backbone 26–54 %, neck 32–54 %, belly 27–45 % ~ | dense, fine weave; carries the species-specific pore pattern; the finished face |
| **grain–corium junction** | — | the plane where the tight surface weave hands over to coarse corium. **This is the plane a finish fails at, and the plane a skive can expose** (see `../durability.md` §2b) |
| **reticular corium** | 80–90 % of hide thickness ✓ | coarse, strong, interwoven fibre bundles; the structural body of the leather |
| **hyaline layer / "shell"** | **equine only** | a dense glassy layer between epidermis and corium, present only over the rump. See `horse.md` |
| **flesh split** | remainder | the lower layer after splitting; carries most of the thickness variation; becomes suede |

**Two facts a pattern maker needs from this table:**

1. **Thickness variation lives in the flesh split, not the grain.** Tannery splitting
   exists precisely to even out butt/shoulder/neck/belly differences ✓. So a
   properly-split leather may be near-uniform in *thickness* while remaining very
   non-uniform in *weave and stretch* — the two are decoupled, and pattern design has
   to respect both.
2. **"Full grain" is a statement about L3, not about L1.** It says nothing about which
   part of the animal you got. Full-grain belly is still belly.

## L4 — Direction

The axis everyone forgets, and the one that decides whether a strap sags.

- **The backbone line** is the reference. Specimens are cut **parallel** or
  **perpendicular** to it.
- Collagen fibres run **predominantly parallel to the backbone** ✓. This produces the
  classic trade-off: **higher tensile strength parallel, higher elongation
  perpendicular** ✓. Confirmed at 15° resolution on sheep garment leather: strength
  peaks parallel to the backbone, elongation peaks perpendicular, and the two trends
  are exactly opposite ~.
- **"Line of least stretch"** ~ = parallel to the spine. Long structural pieces must
  follow it. A strap cut across the flank direction will warp and elongate under load.

**But the neat rule has a large asterisk, and it matters for a cutting layout.**
The direction of maximum and minimum stretch is defined only at the
**Official Sampling Position (OSP, ISO 2418)**. **Across the rest of the hide the
direction of the maxima and minima rotates** ✓ — measured on sheepskin.

> **Pattern consequence:** "cut everything parallel to the backbone" is a *hide-average*
> instruction. It is correct at the OSP and approximate elsewhere. For a part whose
> stretch direction is critical, test the actual piece, not the rule. This is also why
> a nesting plan that rotates long parts to save area is trading away an assumed
> property, not just grain cosmetics.

## Species quick-map

| Species | Regions that matter commercially | The one thing to know |
|---|---|---|
| **bovine** | all nine L1 regions; cuts at L2 | the reference case; all the cut names are bovine unless stated |
| **equine** | front, strip, butt, shell, culatta | the only hide with an extra L3 layer, and it is the most valuable part — see `horse.md` |
| **ovine** | neck, back, belly, flank | softest and most variable; strength varies more within a skin than bovine ✓ |
| **caprine** | as ovine but denser grain | grain layer unusually thick and compact; more elastin than sheep ~ |
| **porcine** | back, belly, jowl | follicle pattern is *through* the thickness, so grain cannot be corrected away |
| **fish / exotic** | head, mid, tail | weakest at the tail in several species ~; direction effects dominate |

## How to write a material line in a spec

The pattern a good spec line follows — **one term per level, plus numbers**:

```
MATERIAL  Bovine (L0) · butt / double butt (L2), cut parallel to backbone (L4),
          grain layer intact, full grain (L3) · 1.2-1.4 mm · veg-tan
```

Bad, and common:

```
MATERIAL  Culatta, 2 mm
```

— two levels, one of them ambiguous, and a thickness that hides the fact that a real
culatta spans several thicknesses in one piece.

## What this taxonomy cannot tell you

- **Which specific hide you have.** Within-hide variation exceeds between-hide variation
  in some studies ✓ and the reverse in others. The map is a prior, not a measurement.
- **Thickness in millimetres for a given cut.** Tannery splitting and shaving set the
  thickness, not the anatomy. Any table of "butt = 4.5 mm, belly = 3 mm" is describing
  unsplit hide, or one particular tannery's routing ~.
- **Whether a defect is present.** That is grading, not anatomy.
