# Amphibians and shelled animals — toad, turtle, and the "not a hide" cases

The tail of the roster, and the place where the trade's vocabulary breaks down hardest. The
useful content here is mostly **negative**: several things sold as "leather" are not skins,
and the framework in this folder does not apply to them.

---

## Cane toad — *Rhinella marina* (syn. *Bufo marinus*)

The only amphibian skin with a real commercial trade. It exists because the cane toad is an
**invasive pest in Australia**, so the raw material is a by-product of control programmes
rather than of husbandry ~.

| Property | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance | cane toad / giant toad; invasive in Australia; supply is a consequence of culling | ✓ |
| Size | **17–27 cm × 17–27 cm** per skin; minimum usable ~17 cm each way | ✓ supplier |
| Thickness | **~0.8 mm average** | ✓ supplier |
| Tanning | mineral and vegetable both offered | ✓ supplier |
| Flexibility | described as **flexible** by suppliers | ✓ supplier |
| Surface | **warty, dry**; distinct ridges above the eyes running down the snout; enlarged **parotoid glands behind each eye**; ventral surface smoother and paler | ✓ |
| Toxicity | the adult skin **and the parotoid glands** are toxic, as are other glands across the back | ✓ |
| Yield | **a pair of shoes needs more than six hides** ~ | ~ |

### The anatomy that matters

**The parotoid glands and the warts are the pattern.** They are not raised follicles like an
ostrich quill and not plates like a scute — they are **glandular swellings in the skin** ✓.
Two consequences:

1. **The glands are the visual feature**, and on a tanned hide the "poison sacks" remain
   distinctive ~. So the dorsal surface is the decorative face and the ventral is plainer.
2. **The material is small and the strong features are large.** On a 17–27 cm skin with
   prominent parotoid glands, a single strap may contain one or two warts and look like a
   section of something, not a whole. Design for **inlay, jewellery, watch straps and
   small-panel accents**, where a fragment is the intended read.

### Handling

The toxicity is a **raw-material issue, not a finished-leather one** — tanning is what makes it
safe to handle. Do not repeat the toad as though the finished skin were hazardous.

**Toad is the smallest material in this folder that still has a real commercial trade.** It is
the honest answer to "how small can leather get?" — and the answer is: small enough that the
object is a mosaic of skins.

## Turtle and tortoise — the case that is not leather

A turtle's shell is **bone**, not skin: the carapace and plastron are skeletal structures, and
the horny scutes on them are keratin plates. What a tannery can process is the **skin of the
neck, limbs and the soft parts**, which is thin and small.

So:

- **"Turtle leather" is a misuse of the word.** You can make leather from the *skin beside* the
  shell; you cannot tan the shell.
- **The scutes are not a surface feature of a hide** — they are attached to bone. This is the
  same category as pangolin and armadillo plates, and it is why these sit outside L3.5: they
  are not *in* the skin, they *are* a separate hard tissue.
- **Trade is heavily restricted** — most sea turtles are CITES Appendix I. This is a legality
  question long before it is an anatomy question.

> **For a pattern maker:** if someone asks for "turtle leather", ask whether they mean
> tortoiseshell (the keratin plate, a different material entirely), or the skin of the limbs and
> neck. The two have nothing in common, and one of them is usually illegal to trade.

## The general rule for "is it a hide?"

| Ask | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a dermis with a collagen fibre network? | it is leather; this folder applies | go to next question |
| Is the hard part **in** the skin (denticle, scute, osteoderm, quill)? | L3.5 applies — `../appendages.md` | — |
| Is the hard part a **separate tissue** attached to bone (turtle scute, pangolin plate, horn)? | **not leather** — it is a plate or keratin product, and it cannot be patterned as a hide | — |
| Is there no skin at all (shell, horn, hoof, antler, bone)? | not leather. Different material, different suppliers, different rules | — |

This table is the boundary of the whole folder, and it is worth stating plainly because the
trade routinely sells **keratin and bone as "exotic leather"** — hoof "leather", horn
"leather", shagreen from shell. They are respectable materials; they are not skins, and nothing
in `properties.md` applies to them.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Cane toad, Rhinella marina (L0) · dorsal, parotoid glands intact (L3.5) ·
          ~0.8 mm · 17-27 cm skin · inlay / small panel
```

## Honest limits

- **Toad data is one supplier's product listing.** Size, thickness and flexibility are that
  seller's, not a standard. The anatomy (warts, parotoid glands, toxicity) is from a general
  reference ✓ and is solid.
- **No mechanical testing exists** for toad leather in anything found. Do not compare it to
  anything numerically.
- **Turtle/tortoise is documented here as a category error**, deliberately, rather than with a
  fabricated property table.
- **Legality** for amphibians and reptiles varies by species and jurisdiction and is not anatomy.
  CITES covers many; national rules cover more.
