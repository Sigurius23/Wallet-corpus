# Porcine — pig, hog, peccary

The one common leather where **the grain is a through-thickness property**.

## The defining fact

Pig hair follicles are **grouped in threes and penetrate the full depth of the hide**.
Almost every species has follicles that stop partway down; a pig's do not. This has three
consequences that no other leather shares:

1. **The grain cannot be corrected away.** You cannot sand, buff or split off the pore
   pattern and get a smooth surface, because the channels run the whole way through. A
   "corrected" pigskin still shows the pattern in cross-section.
2. **"Full grain" is the only option that makes anatomical sense.** There is no top-grain
   / corrected-grain hierarchy to choose from.
3. **The pore pattern is the identification.** Three-pronged pore clusters, evenly spaced,
   visible on every pigskin.

**Peccary** (wild boar, the classic glove leather) shows the same three-prong follicle
pattern, and it is how the trade tells genuine peccary from pigskin imitations ~.

## The regions

Pigskin is **dense, similar to cowhide, soft and supple, with very good durability** ✓.

| Region | Character | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Back / side** | densest, most even | the working area for panels and small goods |
| **Belly** | thinner, looser | linings |
| **Jowl / head** | very distinct deep-grooved grain, small area | decorative, small parts |
| **Shoulder** | intermediate | general |

Be suspicious of any spec that quotes pig regions with bovine precision — the trade
literature on pigskin is much thinner than on cowhide.

## Why it turns up in gloves

- **High lanolin content keeps it soft after wetting** ✓
- Pliable, comfortable, water-resistant ✓
- Used for work gloves, shoes, boots, sportswear, handbags, saddle seats, belts, apparel ✓

For a flat-goods pattern, pigskin's useful properties are **abrasion resistance and
density in a thin substance**; its liability is the deep follicle pattern, which will
telegraph through any thin flexible part and which cannot be skived off.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Porcine (L0) · back (L1) · full grain, follicle pattern retained (L3) ·
          0.8-1.0 mm · garment or glove tannage
```

Note the L3 wording: on pigskin you must state the grain is **retained**, because on every
other leather "corrected grain" is a real option and here it is not.
