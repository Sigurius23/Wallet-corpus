# Snakes and lizards

Small skins, one dominant axis: **dorsal vs ventral**, and one dominant failure mode:
**scale lift**.

---

## The anatomy that decides everything

**Snake scales are not separate plates sitting on the skin. They are folds of the skin
itself, continuous with the tissue between them** ✓. Each is built from **α-keratin beneath
β-keratin** — the same protein family as fingernails ✓.

Three regions:

| Region | Scales | Property |
|---|---|---|
| **Ventral (belly)** | large, wide, **rectangular**, spanning the full body width | smooth, **especially low friction** — built for locomotion. Grip in one direction, slip in the other |
| **Dorsal / costal (back)** | smaller, often teardrop, **overlapping like roof shingles**, rows diagonally offset | pattern, camouflage, temperature |
| **Vertebral** | a special row of larger scales along the very top of the back | — |
| **Head** | plates (scutes) on the head, named by position — preocular, postocular, supraocular | identification, not design |
| **Tail** | beyond the **anal plate** | the anal plate (single or divided) marks the boundary ✓ |

**Mechanical testing has confirmed ventral and dorsal scales differ in stiffness and wear
resistance**, reflecting their different jobs ✓. And under SEM the ventral scales carry rows
of microfibrillar structures that slide forward but resist backward slip ✓.

## The failure mode: scale lift

Because a scale is a **free-edged fold**, its failure is not a crack — it is **lifting and
peeling at the edge**.

The documented case: a **1930 chair seat upholstered in python skin, with some scales
"lifting or loose"** ✓ — and modern reports of scales peeling on snake wallets. This is not
a defect of one tannery; it is the geometry.

Consequences:

| Issue | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Adhesion matters more than strength** | A lifted scale is a delamination, and in `../durability.md` §2c terms this is the **interface** regime — spacing formulae do not apply, the fix is adhesion |
| **Flexing at a fold** | Each scale edge is a potential initiation site. Do not put a snake-skin fold on the outside of a hinge |
| **Dryness accelerates lift** | Scales can peel if the skin dries out ~. Conditioning matters more here than on most leathers |
| **Thin and delicate** | Snake skins are thinner and more delicate than most exotics ~ — line them |

## Snakes vs lizards

| | snake | lizard |
|---|---|---|
| Scale arrangement | overlapping rows, shingled | fine, tightly packed, **pebble-like**, usually non-overlapping |
| Signature pattern | dorsal pattern + ventral rectangles | the **ring mark** — concentric rings of scales, characteristic of ring lizards |
| Skin size | long, narrow — a **tube** flattened out | small; typically a body skin, quite short |
| Usable area | the middle of the skin; head and tail taper | limited |

The **tube shape is the pattern constraint on snake**: the widest part is the mid-body, and
both ends taper. A long straight strap has to come out of the central run, along the body
axis, because that is where the dorsal rows are parallel and the ventral rectangles are
aligned.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Python (L0, species) · mid-body (L1) · dorsal, scales intact (L3.5) ·
          scales aligned along the part axis · 0.6-0.9 mm · lined
```

## Honest limits

- **Vocabulary is herpetological, not leather-trade.** There is no established trade
  taxonomy of snake-skin regions beyond dorsal/ventral/head/tail. Anyone quoting a richer one
  is improvising.
- **Scale counts identify species** — dorsal scale rows at midbody, keeled vs smooth,
  ventral count, head plate arrangement ✓. That is a provenance check, not a design input.
- **Most snakes are CITES-listed**, with species and farm-vs-wild distinctions. Not anatomy.
