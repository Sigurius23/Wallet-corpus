# Bovine — the reference case

Cow, steer, bull, ox, calf, kip, buffalo, bison, zebu. Everything in `../taxonomy.md` and
`../properties.md` is written around this animal, so this file is short: what is specific,
and what differs between the bovines.

## The baseline

- **Nine regions** (cheek, face, shoulder, fore shank, belly, bend, butt, belly middle,
  hind shank) — full table in `../taxonomy.md` L1.
- **The L2 cut names are all bovine** unless a source says otherwise: side, back, crop,
  double shoulder, bend, butt, culatta, offal.
- Strength varies **4–7× within one hide**; peak tensile is **contested** by region.
  See `../properties.md`. Do not reduce this to "the butt is strongest".

## What actually differs between bovines

| Animal | What changes for a pattern maker |
|---|---|
| **Calf / baby calf** | fine grain, thin, the reference for "fine break" (up to 20 wrinkles per inch is *good*). Best for small goods that must look clean |
| **Kip** | between calf and cow; ambiguous across sources — avoid as a spec word |
| **Cow / steer** | the default. Steer hide has a specified **belly line** for grading; cow hide's belly line sits at a different height. A grader's distinction, not a design one |
| **Bull** | **thick, rough head, neck and shoulders; coarse flanks** ✓. Not a bigger steer — different proportions of the map |
| **Buffalo** | thicker fibres, more widely spaced, pronounced pebbly grain, a sub-dermal protective layer; **less flexible than cowhide** at the same substance ✓~ |
| **Bison** | tumbled grain varies **across one side** — the belly has loose large pebbling, the backbone tight ✓. A tannery-run hide is a map, in the strongest sense |
| **Zebu** | neck/hump configuration changes where the hide trims out; leaves a recurring opening or shape near the neck ✓ |

## The practical points

1. **Belly line and breaks are the only normative boundaries.** Everything else is region
   name. If you need precision, cut by the breaks, not by the outline.
2. **Buffalo and bison are not "big cow".** Both are more grained and, for buffalo, less
   flexible at equal thickness. Do not carry a calf pattern across unchanged.
3. **Bison sides are sold as *sides***, so you get the whole map including a loose belly —
   a bison side is a poor candidate for a large flat panel cut without checking grain
   variation first.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Bovine/steer (L0) · butt, double butt (L2) · grain intact (L3) ·
          cut parallel to backbone (L4) · 1.2-1.4 mm · veg-tan
```
