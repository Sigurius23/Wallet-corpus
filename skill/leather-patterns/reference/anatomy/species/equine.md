# Equine — horse, colt, pony

The trade's most structured hide. **Full treatment in `../horse.md`** — front / strip /
butt / shell / culatta, the 460 mm cut, the hyaline layer, and why cordovan rolls instead
of creasing.

This file only records what that one does not.

## The index

| Term | Level | One line |
|---|---|---|
| **front** (FQHH, DHF, SHF) | L2 | forepart, ~2/3 of the hide; 1.0–1.2 mm; coarse marked grain |
| **strip** (horsebutt) | L2 | band in front of the shells; 1.6–4 mm; very coarse grain |
| **shell / cordovan** | L3 | hyaline layer, two ovals per horse, ~1–2.5 sq ft each |
| **culatta** (equine) | L2 | butt leather with shell traces on the flesh side |

## What is specific

- **"Horse butt" is a misnomer.** Strips are cut **in front of** the butt ✓. Ask which of
  strip / shell / whole rear cut is meant — three materials, one phrase.
- **Weight consistency is the weak point.** Horse grain is coarser and there is **large
  weight variation even within one hide**; cowhide is much more consistent and yields
  better ✓. For a repeatable nesting layout this matters more than any aesthetic.
- **Farm status**: horsehide is a **by-product of the meat trade** in countries that eat
  horse ✓. Not farmed for leather; supply is a consequence, not a production plan.
- **Shell is a shape constraint, not just an area constraint.** Two ovals per animal means
  short wide parts. There is no layout that gets a 300 mm continuous run out of a shell.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Equine (L0) · strip / horsebutt, above the shell (L2) · grain (L3) ·
          1.6-2.0 mm · veg-tan
MATERIAL  Equine (L0) · shell cordovan, rump oval (L3) · no grain layer ·
          ~1.5 mm · part must fit within the oval
```
