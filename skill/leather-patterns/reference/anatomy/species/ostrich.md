# Ostrich

**One bird gives four or five different materials.** No other common leather fragments this
hard across a single animal — which makes ostrich the best test of whether the five-level
framework actually works.

Full detail on the hard parts (leg scutes, toe pads, the hoof-like nail) is in
`../appendages.md`. This file is the material map.

---

## The map of one bird

| Cut | L1/L2 origin | Surface | Character | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full quill** | **crown** — central back, where neck meets body, a **diamond-shaped** area | dense raised follicle bumps | soft, oil-rich, the recognisable ostrich | premium exteriors: bags, wallets, boot vamps |
| **Half quill / sparse quill** | **flank transition zones** | asymmetrical, sparser follicles | intermediate, quieter | mid-tier accessories, gussets |
| **Smooth** | body away from the crown — sides, belly | fine even grain, **no follicles at all** | same hand as full quill — it is the same skin — reads discreet | linings, panels, quiet exteriors |
| **Leg skin** | lower limbs | **metatarsal scales**: large scutes in a central column down the front, small fine scales on the sides and back | **the toughest cut** — tighter fibre, holds the sharpest burnished edge, resists scuffing best | watch straps, small panels, boot accents, daily-wear edges |
| **Foot** | the didactyl foot, often sold with the claw attached | dense keratinised toe pads, hoof-like nail | rigid inclusions | decorative termini only — see `../appendages.md` |

**The numbers that drive design:**

- The crown is about **one third of the skin** ✓ and **5–6 sq ft of a ~16 sq ft skin** ✓.
  Full quill is expensive because two thirds of every hide cannot be used for it.
- The crown is graded by dividing it into **four quarters** with two imaginary lines — one
  vertical from the base of the neck to the bottom of the bumped area, one horizontal across
  the widest point ✓. Grade 1 = dense, well-distributed bumps in **all four quarters** with
  minimal scarring; lower grades = thinner coverage, uneven distribution or surface damage ✓.
- **Leg skin** is a narrow strip: typically **~18 in long, 8 in wide at the widest, 4 in at
  the narrowest** ✓; supplier widths quoted **8–15 cm** narrow point, **40–50 cm** long,
  **1.2–1.6 mm** thick ~.
- Body skins run **11–20 sq ft** ~.

## What the quill physically is

An **empty feather follicle**: a keratinised collar around a cavity — a "fat pocket" — where
the quill was anchored ✓. Three consequences:

1. **It is soft, not hard.** Ostrich creases and softens like calf rather than cracking like
   a scaled hide ✓. Do not file it with the reptiles.
2. **The collar is what survives.** Fat removal inside the follicle must be gentle enough not
   to damage the raised collar; damage it and **the bump flattens permanently** ✓.
3. **Bump size tracks age** — older birds give larger, more pronounced follicles, younger
   birds a tighter, more uniform pattern ✓.

## Pattern consequences

| Issue | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Quill distribution is the layout** | On a narrow long part (a watch strap) the follicles must run evenly along the **whole** length. Drift off the crown and the bumps fade out near one end — the classic tell of a strap cut from a poor area ✓ |
| **Skiving** | The bumps sit at different heights, so **a flat skive cuts through the raised ones** ✓. Ostrich needs a more careful skive, or avoid skiving through the quilled face |
| **Flex wear is localised** | Along a vamp crease the quills compress and polish to a smooth band, while the rest of the piece keeps its texture ✓. Place the flex line deliberately, or accept a bald stripe there |
| **Match pairs** | For paired items (gloves, boots) buy legs/hides **from the same bird and the same dye lot** — front scutes vary slightly hide to hide ✓ |
| **Symmetry** | On top-grade work the quills should be **evenly sized, well distributed, and mirrored across a pair** ✓ |
| **Leg is directional** | Large scutes down the **front**, small scales on the sides and back, and the **back of the tarsometatarsus is soft and loose with big folds** ✓. The leg skin is itself a front/back map — the back fold was built to fold, the front was built not to |

## Colouring and finish, briefly

Ostrich takes dye differently in the smooth skin versus the dense quill tissue, which is why
the pattern reads with such contrast ✓. Aniline-dyed quills darken more than the surrounding
skin — used deliberately for a two-toned "vintage" effect ✓. Neither is an anatomy issue;
both are finish routes.

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Ostrich (L0) · crown, full quill (L1/L2) · follicles intact, apex pores present (L3.5) ·
          1.0-1.2 mm · selected so quills run evenly along the part
MATERIAL  Ostrich (L0) · leg skin (L1), front scute column oriented along part (L3.5) ·
          1.2-1.6 mm · claw removed
```

## Honest limits

- Body-hide area (11–20 sq ft) and crown area (5–6 sq ft) are **supplier figures**, not a
  standard ✓~. They vary with bird age and sex.
- Grading is **by the centimetre** and based largely on **scars and blemishes, with penalties
  for poorly developed follicles and undersized skins** ✓ — and the definitions of acceptable
  follicle size and "style" are **vague and often subjective to the tanner or buyer** ✓.
  Do not present a grade as a specification; present it as an opinion with a price attached.
