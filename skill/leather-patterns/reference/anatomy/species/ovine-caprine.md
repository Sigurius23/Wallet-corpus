# Ovine and caprine — sheep and goat

Small skins, and the two behave very differently. **Sheep is the variable one; goat is the
even one.** Get this the wrong way round and you will be generous with the wrong animal.

---

## Ovine — sheep, lamb, and the hair-sheep distinction

### The properties

- **Loose fibrous structure, loose grain, light substance, soft feel** ✓ — the least
  structurally reliable of the common leathers, and the softest
- **The greatest within-skin variability of any species tested** ✓ (ovine > bovine > cervine
  in variability, and lowest in absolute strength)
- **Hair sheep > wool sheep** for mechanical strength ✓ — "bastard" sheep, with short fine
  hair rather than wool, produce better leather because there is **no coarse hair fibre** to
  leave weak channels ~

### What this means for a pattern

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use for linings, soft bags, garment-weight goods | Use for a structural panel or a strap |
| Expect grain looseness as normal | Assume a tight grain will hold a burnish |
| Allow more margin on cut placement | Nest tightly to save area — the weak patches are unpredictable |

**The 2,147-specimen study was on sheepskin**, which is why sheep is over-represented in
`../properties.md`. The 4–7× within-hide variation, the direction sign-flip, and the
springiness of the "butt is strongest" claim are all **ovine** results. Do not import them
to bovine without saying so.

### Sheepskin specifics

- Sold by area **2–9 sq ft** ✓
- Wool sheep can be **sheared** and sold as shearling ✓
- Often suede-finished ✓
- Thickness and tensile maps: strength is **highest near the backbone, falling gradually
  toward the edges; the kidney area is weaker than the neck** ✓

## Caprine — goat, kid

### The properties

- **Coarse fibrous structure varying between butt and belly; thick substance** ✓
- **Grain layer unusually thick and compact** — 24–54 % of total thickness, varying by
  region: butt 24–40 %, backbone 26–54 %, neck 32–54 %, belly 27–45 % ✓
- **Low angle of weave even in the butt** ✓ — this is the mechanism. A low weave angle
  everywhere is why goat reads as "tight" across the whole skin rather than just the back
- **More elastin than sheep, concentrated in neck and backbone** ✓
- Hair follicles **straight and deeply rooted, 0.8–2.9 mm** ✓
- Sold by area **4–8 sq ft** ✓

### The regional result that matters

A study of Iranian goats (cashmere vs hairy, kids vs adults, male vs female) found
**region had a highly significant effect** on physical properties ✓:

| Region | Thickness | Tensile strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Shoulder** | among the highest | **highest — 313.4 kgf/cm², breaking force 31.7 kg** |
| Rib | lower | 294.4 kgf/cm² |
| **Hip** | among the highest | **lowest — 226.6 kgf/cm²** |
| Back | among the highest | — |
| Belt | lower | — |

**Shoulder is the strongest region in goat, and it is also the thickest — while hip is
thick but weak.** That is the opposite of the bovine folk rule, and it is a properly
significant result (P < 0.0001).

Sheep also showed shoulder with higher breaking force than hip or rib ✓ — so for **both**
small ruminants, **shoulder is the strength region**, not the rump.

> **This is the most useful single finding in the species layer.** The "butt/rump is best"
> rule is a *bovine-heavy-hide* heuristic that migrated into small-skin work where it does
> not hold. On a goat or a sheep, look to the shoulder.

### Other caprine facts for a spec

- **Sex matters more than breed type.** Males: heavier skins, greater area, higher breaking
  force (32.9 vs 23.2 kg), higher tensile strength, higher extension ✓. The differences
  between male and female, and between kid and adult, were **greater than** between cashmere
  and hairy goats ✓
- Adults > kids in breaking force and extension, but tensile strength was **not**
  significantly different ✓
- Vegetably tanned goat is used as **lining** ✓
- Goat hair density is high — up to **8,000–18,000 hairs per sq inch** ~ — which is why the
  grain pattern is so fine

## What to specify

```
MATERIAL  Caprine (L0), adult (L0) · shoulder (L1) · grain intact (L3) ·
          0.8-1.0 mm · veg-tan
MATERIAL  Ovine, hair sheep (L0) · back, parallel to backbone (L1/L4) ·
          grain intact · 0.8 mm · for lining
```

## Summary

| | sheep | goat |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | loose, variable | dense, even, low weave angle |
| Strongest region | shoulder (and back) | **shoulder** |
| Weakest | belly, kidney area | hip |
| Use for | linings, soft goods | linings, small goods, bookbinding |
| Main risk | unpredictable weak patches | thickness variation butt↔belly |
